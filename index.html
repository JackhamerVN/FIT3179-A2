THE RECOVERY

STATES & AIRPORTS

Growth in the west.
Weight in the east.

On the same 57 routes, Western Australia grew by 19.5% and the ACT fell by 18.5%. Change is not size.

05

FULLER PLANES

Fewer seats.
Fuller planes.

Airlines flew 2.9% fewer seats than in 2018–19 for almost the same passengers. Seats filled rose from 78.7% to 80.7%.

Why? The ACCC points to the gap left by Tigerair’s exit in 2020, Rex’s regional cuts and late deliveries of new aircraft.

THE TAKEAWAY

The total came back.
The network did not.

Australia flies almost as much as in 2018–19, but with 2.6 million more residents. Mining and holiday routes grew; short south-eastern routes and Melbourne–Sydney shrank. With fewer seats on offer, planes are fuller.

The data

Latest disclosed routes represent 53,982,647 trips, or 88.9% of the national total. National and city-pair statistics include visitors taking domestic trips and domestic stages of Australian international flights. International stages are excluded. Chart 03 compares the growth of this domestic activity with the growth of the resident population; it does not measure how often residents themselves fly. Chart 12 uses BITRE’s separate airport series, whose coverage differs: domestic legs of international services are generally excluded, except international flight numbers operating wholly between domestic airports since July 2011.

Reading the figures

City-pair totals combine both directions. Withheld or unavailable records are not zero. The 57-route comparison uses the same routes in both years. Financial years run from July to June. Airport passengers count each trip at both of its airports. State changes are not adjusted for population. Route seat occupancy weights every route equally; BITRE’s distance-weighted load factor for 2025–26 is 82.2%. Flow-map curves are schematic, not flight paths.
