Domestic passenger trips are almost back to their pre-pandemic total. Behind that national figure, individual routes tell very different stories.

01

THE RECOVERY

Close to the old total.
Not the same journey.

From 60.98 million trips in 2018–19 to 60.75 million in 2025–26. National recovery conceals gains and losses across the network.

02

THE NETWORK

A big country.
A concentrated network.

Melbourne–Sydney alone accounts for 8.08 million trips. The ten busiest disclosed routes carry just over half of the national total.

PEOPLE & AIRPORTS

Similar trip totals.
A larger population.

Australia had 2.64 million more residents at the end of 2025 than at the end of 2018. Passenger trips were 0.4% lower, so trips per resident fell by about a tenth.

THE TAKEAWAY

A national recovery
is not a uniform recovery.

The total is close to 2018–19. The mix of journeys is not. Reading the national trend alongside comparable routes gives a more useful picture of how Australia is flying again.

Sources & scope

The data

BITRE, Australian domestic aviation activity: time series: Top routes workbook, July 2014–June 2026. BITRE, Airport traffic data: passenger movements by airport, 2014–15 to 2025–26 (chart 15). Natural Earth: state boundaries.

Latest disclosed routes represent 53,982,647 trips, or 88.9% of the national total. National and city-pair statistics include visitors taking domestic trips and domestic stages of Australian international flights. International stages are excluded. Chart 14 divides this domestic activity by resident population; it does not isolate residents’ travel. Chart 15 uses BITRE’s separate airport series, whose coverage differs: domestic legs of international services are generally excluded, except international flight numbers operating wholly between domestic airports since July 2011.

Reading the figures

City-pair totals combine both directions. Withheld or unavailable records are not zero. The 57-route comparison uses the same routes in both years. Financial years run from July to June.
